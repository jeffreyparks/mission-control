primary  ·  Measurement Craft

Bayesian A/B Testing Isn't a Free Lunch

Spotify's argument, that 'Bayesian vs. frequentist' is a false frame because Bayesian configurations vary wildly in what they actually guarantee, mirrors a conversation this candidate has had directly: adjudicating conflicting vendor MMM outputs and building a backtesting harness to validate forecast methodology before it reaches planning. The mistake Spotify is calling out (picking a tool before naming the goal) is the same one clients make when they ask for 'incrementality testing' without specifying what error rate or precision they actually need.

Angle

The real failure isn't Bayesian vs. frequentist, it's skipping the step of defining what the experimentation program needs to guarantee (false-positive control, estimation precision, or a cost function) before picking a statistical configuration, which is the same due-diligence gap he's seen when marketing teams shop for a new testing platform.

secondary  ·  Causal Generalizability

When Should a Marketing Mix Model Distrust Itself?

The article's central question, whether relationships learned from Drug A still hold for Drug B, is literally the transferability problem behind MMM and geo-lift work: whether channel elasticities learned in one brand, market, or media environment still apply after the mix, pricing, or seasonality shifts. This candidate has sat exactly at that friction point, reconciling two vendors' MMMs with conflicting channel ROI into a single view for annual budget allocation.

Angle

Vendors defend an MMM by pointing to backtested accuracy, but the harder and more useful question is when the model's underlying assumptions about channel relationships stopped matching the current media environment, not whether last quarter's forecast happened to land.

A Chat Box Doesn't Replace Your Metrics Layer

OpenAI is selling the same promise this candidate already shipped at OMD: let planners run their own scenario and budget questions instead of filing a request. The product's own vendor quotes make clear it only works when it's grounded in a governed semantic layer (dbt, Genie Ontology, BI metric definitions), which is precisely the guardrail-metrics discipline that determines whether self-serve tooling produces trustworthy answers or just faster wrong ones.

Angle

Shipping a self-serve tool doesn't remove the need for a rigorous metric definition layer, it just moves the fight over 'whose number is right' earlier and makes its absence more visible, which is exactly what determined whether his own forecasting app actually cut routine requests or just relocated them.
